## Design Context

### Users

Three groups use the product, with overlapping needs:

- **HR admins and operations managers** configure Xero connections, manage feeds and tokens, review sync health and audit logs, and administer plans and billing. They can work with dense data and need to know exactly what each control does. Their screens include settings, sync, feeds, public holidays, and plans.
- **Team managers** check who is away or working remotely and clear the approvals queue. They visit often and briefly, so the calendar must be quick to scan. Their screens include the calendar, leave approvals, and analytics.
- **Employees** check their own leave, balances, and availability, and submit or withdraw requests. Their screens are less dense and use plainer language. They include the dashboard, availability, leave balances, and notifications.

All three groups get the same quality of design. A screen shared across roles adjusts its density to the role, never below readable size.

### Brand

Team Calendar handles payroll and leave data, so the interface should look accurate and dependable rather than playful. Each screen should answer the user's question quickly and stay out of the way. A typical visit is short: open, find the answer, act, leave.

### Visual Direction

- **Brand colour:** `#46734A`, a deep forest green, used for primary actions, confirmations, and the brand mark. It is not decoration.
- **Surfaces:** light-first, on near-grey neutrals with a faint green tint, with generous spacing.
- **Typography:** Plus Jakarta Sans (Google Fonts), a geometric sans that stays legible at small sizes and in dense tables.
- **Border radius:** `20px` for cards, `16px` for floating surfaces, `14px` for buttons and inputs, and `12px` for chips and small elements. No 4px or 8px radii.
- **Motion:** short and tied to state changes. No decorative animation.
- **Theme:** light by default; dark mode follows the user's preference and receives the same care.
- **Depth:** surface tone separates persistent content. Translucency, blur, and soft shadows are reserved for floating surfaces: modals, popovers, dropdowns, sticky headers, toasts, and mobile sheets. Use borders only when nothing else works. See DESIGN.md for the surface tokens.
- **Avoid Notion's style:** flat, document-like pages with undifferentiated text, weak hierarchy, and low-contrast controls.

### Design Principles

1. **Make data easy to scan.** Calendar and availability data must be readable at a glance, with a clear hierarchy. Prefer the simpler layout.

2. **Use green sparingly.** `#46734A` marks primary actions, success states, and the brand. Do not use it as a background or decoration.

3. **Match density to the role.** Admin and manager screens can be denser; employee screens need more space. Never trade readability for compactness.

4. **Leave room.** Generous padding and clear separation between sections are the default. Crowded screens are harder to trust and to scan.

5. **Show where each record came from.** Sage marks Xero-synced leave and lavender marks manual availability, on calendars, chips, and reports. Provenance always pairs colour with an icon or label.

6. **Keep frost for floating surfaces.** Translucent, blurred surfaces with soft shadows are only for floating UI: overlays, popovers, sticky headers, toasts, and sheets. They never sit behind primary content, dense data, calendars, or form inputs. Every frosted surface uses the neutral surface colour at partial opacity (never a tinted frost), keeps WCAG 2.2 AA contrast over the worst likely content beneath, has an opaque fallback for unsupported browsers, and respects `prefers-reduced-transparency`.

7. **Meet WCAG 2.2 AA at minimum.** All text, interactive elements, and status indicators meet contrast requirements. Colour is never the only way to show status. Every interactive element is reachable by keyboard with a visible 3px focus ring, and the interface respects `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-reduced-transparency`.

### Avoid

- Cream palettes and warm near-white backgrounds common in generic SaaS products.
- Grids of large metric cards and numbered section headings.
- Bright success-green accents, cluttered legacy HR portals, and decoration that competes with the data.

### Language

- Australian English throughout (organise, analyse, colour, centre, prioritise).
- No em dashes. Use commas, colons, semicolons, or parentheses.
- Direct, professional tone. No hype, cliches, or motivational language.
